# Data-Driven Analysis of Musical Form and Harmonic Structure in AI-Generated Popular Music: A Case Study with Suno and Udio

[![License: MIT](https://img.shields.io/badge/License-MIT-yellow.svg)](https://opensource.org/licenses/MIT)

## Abstract
We present a large-scale, data-driven comparison of formal and harmonic structure in music generated by users of two popular AI music generation platforms, Suno and Udio. We address three questions: 1) How does AI-generated music’s harmony relate to human popular music? 2) What structural similarities or differences exist between AI and human-composed music? 3) What special considerations need to be taken into account when applying music information retrieval tools to AI-generated music? We analyse 20,002 Suno tracks, 20,002 Udio tracks, and 20,010 human-composed recordings. For harmonic analysis, we develop a beat-aligned automatic chord estimation pipeline combining source separation, polyphonic MIDI transcription, and functional-harmony labelling. For structural analysis, we compute beat-synchronous chroma, construct self-similarity matrices, detect boundaries via Laplacian segmentation, cluster repeated segments, and apply byte-pair encoding to section labels. We validate our chord estimation method against 83 human-annotated songs from iReal Pro, comparing our approach with librosa methods extended with seventh chord templates. Analysis of ten million chord trigrams reveals that Suno music exhibits strong stylistic characteristics of Western pop chord progressions. At the same time, Udio exhibits more balanced chord quality distributions, resembling those found in human-composed music. Byte-pair encoding analysis reveals that Suno tends toward simple AB repetitions, whereas Udio exhibits the widest range of structural variations. These findings suggest Suno (version 3.5 and below) converges on a narrow harmonic and formal space, whereas Udio shows tendencies more similar to human-composed music. Our work contributes to discussions about creative agency, homogenization, and cultural diversity in AI music. We release beat-level chord annotations, metadata, section boundaries, self-similarity matrices, audio links, and code under an MIT license.

## 📊 Dataset

Our dataset comprises **60,014 tracks** across three sources:

| Collection | Tracks | Avg Duration | Total Duration |
|------------|--------|--------------|----------------|
| Last.fm (Human) | 20,010 | 261.3 ± 182.1s | 1,452 hours |
| Suno (AI) | 20,002 | 175.2 ± 54.6s | 1,085 hours |
| Udio (AI) | 20,002 | 143.2 ± 98.6s | 802 hours |

### Released Annotations

For each track, we provide:
- Beat-level chord annotations
- Structural segmentation boundaries
- Self-similarity matrices
- Style metadata tags
- Audio source links
- MIDI files (harmony and bass)

The repository includes code to reproduce all figures from the paper:

- Top 30 trigram distributions by style
- Chord vocabulary comparison across collections
- Self-similarity matrices with segmentation
- BPE structural pattern heatmaps
- Risk ratio analysis of pop progressions
